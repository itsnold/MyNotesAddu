# The Biological Perspective

> [!summary] What this covers
> The perspective that treats psychology as applied physiology. All mental states reduce to brain activity, neurotransmitters, hormones, and genes. Mind and brain are the same thing (monism).

## Philosophical Foundations

The roots stretch back to ancient Greece, but the real push toward biological psychology came from materialist philosophy.

> [!info] Historical Timeline
> | Thinker | Contribution |
> |---|---|
> | **Hippocrates** | Located mental functions in the brain. Personality tied to bodily "humors." |
> | **René Descartes** (1596-1650) | Mind-body dualism slowed biological psychology, but his work on reflexes started physicalist inquiry. |
> | **Thomas Hobbes** (1588-1679) | Radical materialism. Mental processes are motions in the brain. No immaterial soul. |
> | **La Mettrie** (1748) | *Man a Machine*. Humans are biological machines. Thoughts, feelings, choices are all physical. |

> [!abstract] Monism
> The philosophical foundation. Mind and brain are the same thing, not separate substances. This rejects Cartesian dualism.

## Major Figures (19th Century)

> [!info] Franz Gall & Johann Spurzheim
> Created **phrenology** (reading personality from skull bumps). Now discredited, but the core idea of **localization of function** carried forward into modern neuroscience.

> [!info] Paul Broca (1861)
> Studied patient "Tan Tan" who could only say "tan." Post-mortem analysis revealed damage to left frontal lobe.
> 
> **Broca's area** = speech production
> 
> This established localization of function as scientific fact.

> [!info] Carl Wernicke (1874)
> Identified **Wernicke's area** = language comprehension
> 
> | Area | Location | Function |
> |---|---|---|
> | Broca's | Left frontal lobe | Speech production |
> | Wernicke's | Left temporal lobe | Language comprehension |

> [!info] Sir Charles Sherrington
> Discovered the **synapse** and neural transmission. Showed that behavior is mediated by electrochemical processes.

## Major Figures (Modern Era)

> [!info] Wilder Penfield (1891-1976)
> Mapped motor and sensory cortices by electrically stimulating awake patients' brains during surgery. Created the **homunculus map** showing which brain areas control which body parts.

> [!info] Roger Sperry (1963 Nobel Prize)
> **Split-brain research**. Studied patients whose corpus callosum was severed.
> 
> | Left Hemisphere | Right Hemisphere |
> |---|---|
> | Language | Spatial processing |
> | Logic | Holistic thinking |
> | Analytical | Pattern recognition |

> [!info] Eric Kandel (2000 Nobel Prize)
> Showed that learning changes synaptic structure at the molecular level. Used sea slugs to demonstrate that memory formation involves physical changes in neurons.

> [!info] Joseph LeDoux
> Mapped the **amygdala** and fear circuits. Showed that subcortical structures can trigger emotional responses faster than conscious thought.

## Main Tenets

> [!abstract] Reductionism
> All psychological phenomena can be reduced to biological events:
> 
> | Experience | Biological Basis |
> |---|---|
> | Love | Oxytocin and dopamine in reward circuits |
> | Depression | Serotonin depletion, hippocampal atrophy |
> | Memory | Long-term potentiation in synapses |
> | Schizophrenia | Dopamine dysregulation, enlarged ventricles |

> [!info] Localization of Function
> Different brain structures handle different functions:
> 
> | Region | Function |
> |---|---|
> | **Prefrontal cortex** | Executive function, planning, impulse control |
> | **Amygdala** | Fear detection, threat assessment |
> | **Hippocampus** | Memory consolidation, spatial navigation |
> | **Hypothalamus** | Homeostasis, hunger, thirst, sex drive |
> | **Cerebellum** | Motor coordination, procedural learning |

> [!info] Neurochemistry
> Mental states depend on chemical messengers:
> 
> **Neurotransmitters:**
> | Chemical | Role |
> |---|---|
> | Serotonin | Mood regulation |
> | Dopamine | Reward, motivation |
> | GABA | Inhibition |
> | Glutamate | Excitation |
> | Norepinephrine | Arousal |
> 
> **Hormones:**
> | Chemical | Role |
> |---|---|
> | Cortisol | Stress response |
> | Testosterone/Estrogen | Sexual behavior, aggression |
> | Insulin | Metabolism, cognition |

> [!info] Genetic Predisposition
> | Approach | Method |
> |---|---|
> | **Behavioral Genetics** | Twin/adoption studies to calculate heritability |
> | **Molecular Genetics** | Identifying specific genes (DRD4 for novelty-seeking, 5-HTTLPR for depression vulnerability) |
> | **Evolutionary Psychology** | Traits selected for ancestral survival |

## Methods

> [!info] Research Techniques
> | Method | What It Reveals | Famous Example |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Lesion Studies** | Function of damaged areas | Phineas Gage (frontal lobe damage changed personality) |
> | **Brain Imaging** (fMRI, PET, EEG) | Real-time activity during tasks | Amygdala activation during fear |
> | **Twin/Adoption Studies** | Heritability of traits | Minnesota Twin Study (50%+ concordance for personality) |
> | **Psychopharmacology** | Effects of chemical manipulation | SSRIs reducing depression |
> | **Optogenetics** | Causal manipulation of neurons | Controlling behavior with light in mice |
> | **Brain Stimulation** | Direct activation/inhibition | Deep brain stimulation for Parkinson's |

## Modern Updates

The perspective has evolved beyond strict genetic determinism.

> [!abstract] Neuroplasticity
> The brain rewires itself throughout life.
> 
> **London taxi driver study:** Drivers who memorized the city's layout showed enlarged posterior hippocampi. Experience physically changed brain structure.

> [!abstract] Epigenetics
> Environment changes gene expression without altering DNA.
> 
> | Factor | Effect |
> |---|---|
> | Stress, trauma | Can flip genetic switches on/off |
> | These changes | Can be inherited transgenerationally via methylation |
> 
> Nature works *through* nurture. Genes provide potential; environment shapes expression.

> [!abstract] Emergent Property
> Consciousness arises from neural complexity. 100 billion neurons firing in patterns create subjective experience. No "ghost in the machine."

## Criticisms

> [!warning] The Homunculus Fallacy
> Saying "the amygdala did it" pushes the explanation back one level without explaining agency. It's like saying a little man inside your head decided.

> [!warning] Reductionism Loses Meaning
> Love "is" oxytocin chemically. But love as sacrifice, commitment, and connection can't be captured in a chemical formula. Critics argue something essential is lost when you reduce experience to neurons.

> [!warning] Determinism and Ethics
> If behavior is biologically caused, what about moral responsibility? The "my brain made me do it" defense challenges free will.

> [!warning] Correlation vs. Causation
> Brain imaging shows *where* activity occurs during a task. But does that activity *cause* the experience, or just correlate with it?

## Biopsychosocial Integration

Modern psychology rarely uses this perspective alone. It's the "bio" in **biopsychosocial**.

> [!info] Three Components
> | Component | Contribution |
> |---|---|
> | **Biology** | Sets parameters (temperament, predispositions, limits) |
> | **Psychology** | Fills content (cognition, learning, appraisals) |
> | **Social** | Triggers expression (culture, relationships, context) |

> [!tip] Example: Depression
> - **Biological:** Genetic vulnerability, neurotransmitter imbalance
> - **Psychological:** Negative cognitive schemas
> - **Social:** Childhood abuse, social isolation
> 
> The biological perspective provides the hardware. Other perspectives provide the software and user input.

---

> [!abstract] The Bottom Line
> This perspective gave psychology scientific legitimacy by making mental states measurable through brain scans, blood tests, and genetic sequencing. Its greatest triumph is mapping the neural basis of memory and emotion. Its greatest philosophical challenge is the risk of reducing Shakespeare to sodium channels.
