## From Frieze to Wallpaper

Frieze patterns repeat in **one direction**. Wallpaper patterns repeat in **two independent directions**, filling the entire plane.

> [!info] Progression of Pattern Types
> | Pattern Type | Repetition | Number of Types |
> |---|---|:---:|
> | Rosette | None (centered) | Infinite ($C_n$ or $D_n$) |
> | Frieze | One direction | 7 |
> | Wallpaper | Two directions | 17 |
> 
> This progression represents increasing levels of symmetry.

## Translational Symmetry in 2D

> [!definition] Lattice
> A wallpaper pattern is built on a **lattice** — a grid of points created by two independent translation vectors $\vec{v_1}$ and $\vec{v_2}$.
> 
> Every point $P$ in the pattern can be mapped to another identical point by:
> $$P' = P + m\vec{v_1} + n\vec{v_2}$$
> where $m$ and $n$ are integers.

> [!info] Five Lattice Types
> The shape of the basic tile (the **unit cell**) determines the lattice type:
> 
> | Lattice | Shape of Unit Cell | Angle | Side Lengths |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **Oblique** | Parallelogram | $\neq 90°$ | $a \neq b$ |
> | **Rectangular** | Rectangle | $90°$ | $a \neq b$ |
> | **Centered Rectangular** | Rhombus or centered rectangle | Special | Special |
> | **Square** | Square | $90°$ | $a = b$ |
> | **Hexagonal** | Rhombus with $60°$ angle | $60°$ | $a = b$ |
> 
> The lattice type constrains which symmetries are possible.

## The 17 Wallpaper Groups

There are exactly **17 distinct wallpaper groups**, proven mathematically by Evgraf Fedorov in 1891. Every repeating 2D pattern belongs to one of these 17 groups.

> [!abstract] The 17 Wallpaper Groups Overview
> 
> **Rotation only (no reflections):**
> | Group | Highest Rotation | Description |
> |---|---|---|
> | **p1** | None ($360°$) | Translation only |
> | **p2** | $180°$ | Half-turn rotations |
> | **p3** | $120°$ | Three-fold rotations |
> | **p4** | $90°$ | Four-fold rotations |
> | **p6** | $60°$ | Six-fold rotations |
> 
> **With reflections:**
> | Group | Mirror Lines | Rotation | Glide Refl. |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **pm** | Parallel | None | No |
> | **pg** | None | None | Yes |
> | **cm** | Parallel | None | Yes |
> | **pmm** | Perpendicular | $180°$ | No |
> | **pmg** | One direction | $180°$ | Yes |
> | **pgg** | None | $180°$ | Yes |
> | **cmm** | Perpendicular | $180°$ | Yes |
> | **p3m1** | $120°$ angles | $120°$ | — |
> | **p31m** | $120°$ angles | $120°$ | — |
> | **p4m** | $45°/90°$ | $90°$ | — |
> | **p4g** | $45°$ | $90°$ | Yes |
> | **p6m** | $30°/60°$ | $60°$ | — |

> [!tip] Why Exactly 17?
> This isn't arbitrary — it's a mathematical theorem. The constraints of repeating in 2D only allow rotational symmetries of order 1, 2, 3, 4, or 6. You **cannot** have 5-fold or 7-fold rotational symmetry in a wallpaper pattern. This is called the **crystallographic restriction theorem**.
> 
> (This is why you never see regular pentagons in bathroom tiles — they can't tile the plane periodically!)

## Classifying Wallpaper Patterns

> [!tip] Classification Flowchart
> **Step 1: What is the highest order of rotation?**
> - No rotation → p1, pm, pg, cm
> - $180°$ only → p2, pmm, pmg, pgg, cmm
> - $120°$ → p3, p3m1, p31m
> - $90°$ → p4, p4m, p4g
> - $60°$ → p6, p6m
> 
> **Step 2: Are there reflections?**
> - No reflections and no glide reflections → p1, p2, p3, p4, p6
> - Has reflections → continue narrowing down
> 
> **Step 3: Are there glide reflections?**
> - Distinguishes between closely related groups (e.g., pm vs. pg)

> [!example] Identifying a Brick Wall Pattern
> A standard brick wall (staggered rows):
> 
> - **Translation:** In two directions (horizontal and diagonal) ✓
> - **Rotation:** $180°$ at center of each brick ✓
> - **Reflection:** No mirror lines ✗
> - **Glide reflection:** Yes (shift half-brick horizontally, reflect vertically) ✓
> 
> **Classification: pgg** — two perpendicular glide reflections and $180°$ rotation.

> [!example] Identifying a Checkerboard
> A standard checkerboard pattern (alternating black and white squares):
> 
> - **Translation:** In two perpendicular directions ✓
> - **Rotation:** $90°$ at corners, $180°$ at centers ✓
> - **Reflection:** Along rows, columns, and diagonals ✓
> - **Glide reflection:** Yes ✓
> 
> **Classification: p4m** — all the symmetries of the square lattice.

## Applications and Examples

Wallpaper patterns appear everywhere in human design and nature.

> [!info] Historical and Cultural Examples
> - **Islamic Art:** The Alhambra palace in Granada, Spain contains examples of all 17 wallpaper groups — the only known place where this occurs
> - **M.C. Escher:** The Dutch artist systematically explored all 17 types in his tessellation artworks
> - **Chinese Lattice:** Traditional window patterns in Chinese architecture use many wallpaper groups
> - **African Textiles:** Kente cloth and other African textile traditions use complex wallpaper symmetries

> [!info] Scientific Applications
> - **Crystallography:** The 17 wallpaper groups are the 2D analog of the 230 space groups used to classify crystal structures in 3D
> - **Materials Science:** Understanding symmetry groups helps predict material properties
> - **Architecture:** Tile design and structural patterns rely on these classifications

> [!tip] Connecting the Modules
> This is where Modules 3 and 4 come together:
> - **Module 3** gave us the individual transformations (rotation, reflection, translation, glide reflection)
> - **Module 4** classifies patterns based on which *combinations* of those transformations leave the pattern unchanged
> - Rosettes use rotation and reflection
> - Friezes add translation in one direction
> - Wallpapers add translation in two directions
