## What is a Transformation

Mathematics extends its interpretation to the arts, analyzing patterns, symmetry, and movement. A transformation is essentially a way to move or change points on a plane.

> [!definition] Transformation
> A **transformation** of the plane is a function that maps points (called the **pre-image**) to new points (called the **image**).
> 
> If $P(x, y)$ is a point, then the transformation produces $P'(x', y')$, the image of $P$.
> 
> The relationship is typically expressed as a matrix multiplication:
> $$P' = M \cdot P$$
> where $M$ is the **transformation matrix**.

> [!tip] Coordinate Matrix
> When transforming shapes (not just single points), arrange all vertices as columns in a **coordinate matrix**:
> $$M = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 & x_2 & x_3 & \cdots \\ y_1 & y_2 & y_3 & \cdots \end{bmatrix}$$
> Then multiply the transformation matrix by $M$ to get all new coordinates at once.

## Rigid vs Non-Rigid

Transformations fall into two broad categories based on what they preserve.

> [!info] Rigid Transformation (Isometry)
> Preserves **shape**, **size**, **distance**, and **angles**. The figure looks exactly the same, just in a different position or orientation.
> 
> Types:
> - **Translation** — sliding
> - **Rotation** — turning
> - **Reflection** — flipping
> - **Glide Reflection** — flip + slide
> 
> An isometry can be **proper** (preserves orientation, like translation and rotation) or **improper** (reverses orientation, like reflection).

> [!info] Non-Rigid Transformation
> May change the **size** or **shape** of the figure.
> 
> Types:
> - **Dilation** — scaling (enlargement or reduction)
> - **Shear** — skewing (area is preserved but angles change)

> [!tip] Intuition: Rigid vs. Non-Rigid
> Think of a **rigid** transformation like moving a **hardcover book** — it stays the same shape no matter where you slide or spin it.
> 
> Think of a **non-rigid** transformation like stretching a **rubber band** or projecting a movie onto a curved screen — the dimensions change.

## Dilation

Dilation changes the size of a figure based on a **scale factor** $k$, centered at the origin.

> [!definition] Dilation
> - $k > 1$: **Enlargement** (stretches outward from origin)
> - $0 < k < 1$: **Reduction** (compresses toward origin)
> - $k = 1$: Identity (no change)
> - $k < 0$: Reflects through the origin and scales

> [!abstract] Dilation Matrix
> $$D_k = \begin{bmatrix} k & 0 \\ 0 & k \end{bmatrix}$$
> 
> **Rule:** $(x, y) \to (kx, ky)$
> 
> Every coordinate is simply multiplied by $k$.

> [!example] Walkthrough: Enlargement ($k = 3$)
> **Problem:** Dilate polygon vertices $A(-1,4), B(-3,6), C(-2,8), D(-6,7), E(-5,3), F(-4,2)$ by $k = 3$.
> 
> **Step 1: Set up the Point Matrix.**
> Collate vertices as columns:
> $$P = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & -3 & -2 & -6 & -5 & -4 \\ 4 & 6 & 8 & 7 & 3 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$$
> 
> **Step 2: Multiply by Dilation Matrix.**
> $$D_3 \cdot P = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} -1 & \cdots \\ 4 & \cdots \end{bmatrix}$$
> 
> This simply multiplies every $x$ and $y$ coordinate by 3.
> 
> **Step 3: Calculate Result.**
> $$P' = \begin{bmatrix} -3 & -9 & -6 & -18 & -15 & -12 \\ 12 & 18 & 24 & 21 & 9 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$$
> 
> **New Vertices:** $A'(-3,12), B'(-9,18), C'(-6,24), D'(-18,21), E'(-15,9), F'(-12,6)$

> [!example] Walkthrough: Reduction ($k = 1/2$)
> **Problem:** Dilate quadrilateral $A(-8,12), B(-4,6), C(6,10), D(10,4)$ by $k = 1/2$.
> 
> **Working it out:**
> Multiply every coordinate by $0.5$:
> 
> - $A(-8, 12) \to A'(-4, 6)$
> - $B(-4, 6) \to B'(-2, 3)$
> - $C(6, 10) \to C'(3, 5)$
> - $D(10, 4) \to D'(5, 2)$

## Reflection

A reflection flips a figure over a line (called the **line of reflection** or **mirror line**). It preserves size but **reverses orientation** — like looking in a mirror.

> [!abstract] Standard Reflection Matrices
> All reflection matrices have **determinant = $-1$** (orientation is reversed).
> 
> | Mirror Line | Matrix | Rule |
> |---|---|---|
> | x-axis ($y = 0$) | $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$ | $(x, y) \to (x, -y)$ |
> | y-axis ($x = 0$) | $\begin{bmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ | $(x, y) \to (-x, y)$ |
> | Line $y = x$ | $\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ | $(x, y) \to (y, x)$ |
> | Line $y = -x$ | $\begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ | $(x, y) \to (-y, -x)$ |
> | Origin | $\begin{bmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$ | $(x, y) \to (-x, -y)$ |
> 
> *Note: Origin reflection is the same as $180°$ rotation.*

> [!example] Walkthrough: Reflection over y-axis
> **Problem:** Reflect quadrilateral $A(-3,-2), B(-2,-5), C(-6,-5), D(-6,-2)$ over the **y-axis**.
> 
> **Matrix:** $\begin{bmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$
> 
> **Working it out:**
> Multiply $x$-coordinates by $-1$. Keep $y$-coordinates the same.
> 
> - $A(-3, -2) \to A'(3, -2)$
> - $B(-2, -5) \to B'(2, -5)$
> - $C(-6, -5) \to C'(6, -5)$
> - $D(-6, -2) \to D'(6, -2)$

> [!example] Walkthrough: Reflection over $y = -x$
> **Problem:** Reflect the same quadrilateral over $y = -x$.
> 
> **Rule:** Swap coordinates and negate both. $(x, y) \to (-y, -x)$.
> 
> **Working it out:**
> - $A(-3, -2) \to A'(2, 3)$
> - $B(-2, -5) \to B'(5, 2)$
> - $C(-6, -5) \to C'(5, 6)$
> - $D(-6, -2) \to D'(2, 6)$

## Rotation

Turns a figure around a fixed point (usually the origin) by an angle $\theta$.

> [!info] Direction Convention
> - **Counterclockwise (CCW):** Positive $\theta$
> - **Clockwise (CW):** Negative $\theta$
> - **Equivalence:** $270°$ CCW = $90°$ CW

> [!abstract] Rotation Matrix
> $$R_\theta = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix}$$
> 
> **Determinant is always 1** (preserves area and orientation).
> 
> **Common Angle Shortcuts (CCW):**
> 
> | Angle | Matrix | Rule |
> |---|---|---|
> | $90°$ | $\begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ | $(x, y) \to (-y, x)$ |
> | $180°$ | $\begin{bmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$ | $(x, y) \to (-x, -y)$ |
> | $270°$ | $\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ | $(x, y) \to (y, -x)$ |
> 
> **Clockwise:** $CW(\theta) = CCW(-\theta) = CCW(360° - \theta)$

> [!example] Walkthrough: Rotation by $90°$ CCW
> **Problem:** Rotate $P(-1,2), Q(-4,1), R(-6,3), S(-4,5)$ by $90°$ CCW.
> 
> **Rule:** $(x, y) \to (-y, x)$
> 
> **Working it out:**
> - $P(-1, 2) \to P'(-2, -1)$
> - $Q(-4, 1) \to Q'(-1, -4)$
> - $R(-6, 3) \to R'(-3, -6)$
> - $S(-4, 5) \to S'(-5, -4)$

> [!example] Walkthrough: Rotation by $270°$ CCW
> **Problem:** Rotate $A(2,3), B(6,7), C(5,2), D(3,6)$ by $270°$.
> 
> **Rule:** $(x, y) \to (y, -x)$
> 
> **Working it out:**
> - $A(2, 3) \to A'(3, -2)$
> - $B(6, 7) \to B'(7, -6)$
> - $C(5, 2) \to C'(2, -5)$
> - $D(3, 6) \to D'(6, -3)$

## Translation

Slides every point by the same vector $\vec{v} = (h, k)$. This is the simplest isometry.

> [!definition] Translation
> **Rule:** $(x, y) \to (x + h, y + k)$
> 
> Translation is an isometry but it is **not a linear transformation** — it cannot be represented by a $2 \times 2$ matrix because it involves addition, not multiplication.

> [!tip] Two Methods for Translation
> **Method 1: Vector Addition** (simple and direct)
> $$P' = P + \vec{v}$$
> $$x' = x + h, \quad y' = y + k$$
> 
> **Method 2: Homogeneous Coordinates** (needed for composite transformations)
> 
> Extend to $3 \times 3$ matrices by adding a third coordinate = 1:
> $$T_{h,k} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & h \\ 0 & 1 & k \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad P = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

> [!example] Walkthrough: Translation
> **Problem:** Translate pentagon $A(1,2), B(4,2), C(5,4), D(3,5), E(1,4)$ by vector $(2, -3)$.
> 
> **Working it out:**
> Add 2 to every $x$, subtract 3 from every $y$:
> 
> - $A(1, 2) \to A'(3, -1)$
> - $B(4, 2) \to B'(6, -1)$
> - $C(5, 4) \to C'(7, 1)$
> - $D(3, 5) \to D'(5, 2)$
> - $E(1, 4) \to E'(3, 1)$
