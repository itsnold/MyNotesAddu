## What is a Wallpaper Pattern

> [!definition] Wallpaper Pattern
> A **wallpaper pattern** is a two-dimensional repeating pattern that covers an infinite plane without gaps or overlaps. It exhibits **translational symmetry** in two independent directions and may also include rotations, reflections, and glide reflections.

The **International Union of Crystallography (IUC) Notation** classifies every possible wallpaper pattern into one of exactly **17 distinct groups** based on its symmetry properties (lattice type, rotation order, mirror reflections, and glide reflections).

> [!tip] Why Only 17?
> It has been mathematically proven that there are exactly 17 fundamentally different ways to repeat a motif across a plane. Every wallpaper, tile floor, fabric pattern, or Islamic geometric design falls into one of these 17 categories.

## Building Blocks

To classify wallpaper patterns, we need three foundational concepts: rotation centers, lattices, and unit cells.

### Rotation Centers

A rotation center is a point around which the pattern can be rotated and still look identical.

> [!abstract] Rotation Orders
> | Order | Angle | Description |
> |:---:|:---:|---|
> | 1 | $360°$ | Trivial (every pattern has this) |
> | 2 | $180°$ | Half-turn |
> | 3 | $120°$ | One-third turn |
> | 4 | $90°$ | Quarter-turn |
> | 6 | $60°$ | One-sixth turn |

> [!warning] No Order 5!
> Order 5 rotation ($72°$) **cannot** tile the plane. Only orders 1, 2, 3, 4, and 6 produce valid wallpaper patterns. This is a fundamental constraint of 2D crystallography.

### Lattices

A **lattice** is a repeating arrangement of points in a plane that defines the underlying structure of a pattern. There are **5 types**:

> [!info] The Five Lattice Types
> | Lattice | Description | Key Feature |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Oblique** | No perpendicular or equal sides | Most general, skewed |
> | **Rectangular** | Perpendicular but unequal sides | Forms rectangles |
> | **Square** | Perpendicular and equal sides | Forms a grid of squares |
> | **Hexagonal** | $120°$ angles between points | Honeycomb-like structure |
> | **Rhombic** | Centered rectangular | Forms diamond shapes |

> [!tip] Primitive vs. Centered Lattices
> - **Primitive (p):** The smallest repeating unit has lattice points only at its corners. One lattice point per unit cell.
> - **Centered (c):** The unit cell has additional lattice points inside (at the center or along edges). This is why some groups start with "c" instead of "p".

### Unit Cell

> [!definition] Unit Cell
> The **unit cell** is the smallest repeating structure (fundamental region) that, when copied and shifted in two directions, can generate the entire pattern. It is the building block of the lattice.

```tikz
\begin{document}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.7]
    % Grid of dots
    \foreach \x in {0,1.5,3,4.5,6} {
        \foreach \y in {0,1.5,3,4.5} {
            \fill[gray] (\x,\y) circle (2pt);
        }
    }
    % Highlight one unit cell
    \draw[thick, red, fill=red!10] (1.5,1.5) rectangle (3,3);
    \node[font=\small, red!70!black] at (2.25, 2.25) {Unit Cell};
    % Arrows for translation vectors
    \draw[->, thick, blue] (1.5,1.5) -- (3,1.5) node[midway, below, font=\small] {$\vec{a}$};
    \draw[->, thick, blue] (1.5,1.5) -- (1.5,3) node[midway, left, font=\small] {$\vec{b}$};
    \node[font=\small] at (3,-0.7) {Lattice points with one unit cell highlighted};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{document}
```

## The 17 Wallpaper Groups

Each group is described by its IUC notation, symmetry properties, and a TikZ sample showing how a simple motif (the letter **F**) repeats under that group's symmetries. The F is asymmetric, making it easy to spot reflections and rotations.

### No Rotation Groups (Order 1)

These groups have no rotational symmetry beyond the trivial $360°$.

#### p1 — Translation Only

```tikz
\begin{document}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.7]
    \foreach \x in {0,1.2,2.4,3.6,4.8} {
        \foreach \y in {0,1,2,3} {
            \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=blue!70!black] at (\x, \y) {F};
        }
    }
    \node[font=\small, gray] at (2.4,-0.7) {p1: pure translation, no symmetry};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{document}
```

> [!info] p1 Properties
> - **Lattice:** Oblique (Primitive)
> - **Rotation:** None (Order 1)
> - **Mirrors:** 0 | **Glides:** 0
> - The simplest possible wallpaper group. The motif repeats by shifting only.

#### pg — Glide Reflection

```tikz
\begin{document}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.7]
    \foreach \x in {0,1.4,2.8,4.2} {
        \foreach \y in {0,2} {
            \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=blue!70!black] at (\x, \y) {F};
        }
        \foreach \y in {1,3} {
            \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=red!70!black, xscale=-1] at (\x+0.7, \y) {F};
        }
    }
    \draw[dashed, gray!60] (-0.5,0.5) -- (5.5,0.5);
    \draw[dashed, gray!60] (-0.5,1.5) -- (5.5,1.5);
    \draw[dashed, gray!60] (-0.5,2.5) -- (5.5,2.5);
    \node[font=\small, gray] at (2.4,-0.7) {pg: alternating rows are shifted \& reflected};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{document}
```

> [!info] pg Properties
> - **Lattice:** Rectangular
> - **Rotation:** None
> - **Mirrors:** 0 | **Glides:** 1
> - Alternating rows are shifted and reflected (glide reflection). Common in brickwork and woven textiles.

#### pm — Mirror Reflection

```tikz
\begin{document}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.7]
    \foreach \x in {0,2.6,5.2} {
        \foreach \y in {0,1,2,3} {
            \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=blue!70!black] at (\x, \y) {F};
            \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=red!70!black, xscale=-1] at (\x+1.3, \y) {F};
        }
    }
    \draw[dashed, gray] (0.65,-0.3) -- (0.65,3.4);
    \draw[dashed, gray] (3.25,-0.3) -- (3.25,3.4);
    \draw[dashed, gray] (5.85,-0.3) -- (5.85,3.4);
    \node[font=\small, gray] at (2.8,-0.7) {pm: mirror lines shown dashed};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{document}
```

> [!info] pm Properties
> - **Lattice:** Rectangular
> - **Rotation:** None
> - **Mirrors:** 1 axis | **Glides:** 0
> - Columns are mirrored along vertical axes (dashed lines).

#### cm — Mirror + Glide

```tikz
\begin{document}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.7]
    \foreach \x in {0,2.6} {
        \foreach \y in {0,2} {
            \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=blue!70!black] at (\x, \y) {F};
            \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=red!70!black, xscale=-1] at (\x+1.3, \y) {F};
        }
        \foreach \y in {1,3} {
            \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=blue!70!black] at (\x+0.65, \y) {F};
            \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=red!70!black, xscale=-1] at (\x+1.95, \y) {F};
        }
    }
    \draw[dashed, gray] (0.65,-0.3) -- (0.65,3.4);
    \node[font=\small, gray] at (2.4,-0.7) {cm: mirror + staggered rows (glide)};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{document}
```

> [!info] cm Properties
> - **Lattice:** Centered Rectangular
> - **Rotation:** None
> - **Mirrors:** 1 | **Glides:** 1
> - Mirror lines and glide reflections alternate between rows. Visually balanced and flowing.

### Half-Turn Groups (Order 2)

These groups have $180°$ rotational symmetry.

#### p2 — $180°$ Rotation Only

```tikz
\begin{document}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.7]
    \foreach \x in {0,1.6,3.2,4.8} {
        \foreach \y in {0,2} {
            \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=blue!70!black] at (\x, \y) {F};
        }
        \foreach \y in {1,3} {
            \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=red!70!black, rotate=180] at (\x+0.8, \y) {F};
        }
    }
    \node[font=\small, gray] at (2.4,-0.7) {p2: half-turn symmetry, no mirrors};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{document}
```

> [!info] p2 Properties
> - **Lattice:** Oblique
> - **Rotation:** $180°$ (Order 2)
> - **Mirrors:** 0 | **Glides:** 0
> - Half-turn symmetry only. Motifs look the same when flipped upside down, but there are no mirror lines.

#### pmm — $180°$ + 2 Mirrors

```tikz
\begin{document}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.7]
    \foreach \x in {0,3.2} {
        \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=blue!70!black] at (\x, 1.3) {F};
        \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=red!70!black, xscale=-1] at (\x+1.6, 1.3) {F};
        \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=green!50!black, yscale=-1] at (\x, 0) {F};
        \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=orange!80!black, xscale=-1, yscale=-1] at (\x+1.6, 0) {F};
    }
    \draw[dashed, gray] (0.8, -0.5) -- (0.8, 2.0);
    \draw[dashed, gray] (4.0, -0.5) -- (4.0, 2.0);
    \draw[dashed, gray] (-0.5, 0.65) -- (5.5, 0.65);
    \node[font=\small, gray] at (2.4,-1.0) {pmm: horizontal + vertical mirrors};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{document}
```

> [!info] pmm Properties
> - **Lattice:** Rectangular
> - **Rotation:** $180°$ (Order 2)
> - **Mirrors:** 2 (horizontal + vertical) | **Glides:** 0
> - Classic tiled floor pattern. Highly symmetrical with perpendicular mirror axes.

#### pmg — $180°$ + Mirror + Glide

> [!info] pmg Properties
> - **Lattice:** Rectangular
> - **Rotation:** $180°$ (Order 2)
> - **Mirrors:** 1 | **Glides:** 1
> - Dynamic flow maintained by glide reflection while retaining $180°$ rotational balance.

#### pgg — $180°$ + 2 Glides

> [!info] pgg Properties
> - **Lattice:** Rectangular
> - **Rotation:** $180°$ (Order 2)
> - **Mirrors:** 0 | **Glides:** 2
> - No standard mirror lines, but repeats via shifted reflections (glides) in two directions.

#### cmm — $180°$ + 2 Mirrors + 2 Glides

> [!info] cmm Properties
> - **Lattice:** Centered Rectangular / Rhombic
> - **Rotation:** $180°$ (Order 2)
> - **Mirrors:** 2 | **Glides:** 2
> - Both vertical/horizontal mirrors and glides, creating highly balanced ornamental designs.

---

### Quarter-Turn Groups (Order 4)

These groups have $90°$ rotational symmetry and always use a **square lattice**.

#### p4 — $90°$ Rotation Only

```tikz
\begin{document}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.65]
    \foreach \x in {0,2.2,4.4} {
        \foreach \y in {0,2.2} {
            \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=blue!70!black] at (\x, \y+0.55) {F};
            \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=red!70!black, rotate=90] at (\x+0.55, \y) {F};
            \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=green!50!black, rotate=180] at (\x+1.1, \y+0.55) {F};
            \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=orange!80!black, rotate=270] at (\x+0.55, \y+1.1) {F};
        }
    }
    \node[font=\small, gray] at (2.75,-0.7) {p4: four copies at 0$^\circ$, 90$^\circ$, 180$^\circ$, 270$^\circ$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{document}
```

> [!info] p4 Properties
> - **Lattice:** Square
> - **Rotation:** $90°$ (Order 4)
> - **Mirrors:** 0 | **Glides:** 0
> - Pinwheel-like pattern. Four copies of the motif rotated by $90°$ around each center.

#### p4m — $90°$ + 4 Mirrors

> [!info] p4m Properties
> - **Lattice:** Square
> - **Rotation:** $90°$ (Order 4)
> - **Mirrors:** 4 (horizontal, vertical, both diagonals) | **Glides:** 0
> - Kaleidoscope grid. The most symmetrical square-lattice group. Resembles highly structured tile patterns.

#### p4g — $90°$ + 2 Mirrors + 2 Glides

> [!info] p4g Properties
> - **Lattice:** Square
> - **Rotation:** $90°$ (Order 4)
> - **Mirrors:** 2 (diagonal) | **Glides:** 2

### Third-Turn Groups (Order 3)

These groups have $120°$ rotational symmetry and always use a **hexagonal lattice**.

#### p3 — $120°$ Rotation Only

```tikz
\begin{document}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.65]
    \foreach \x in {0,2.4,4.8} {
        \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=blue!70!black] at (\x, 0) {F};
        \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=red!70!black, rotate=120] at (\x+0.6, 0.4) {F};
        \node[font=\small\bfseries, text=green!50!black, rotate=240] at (\x+0.3, -0.5) {F};
    }
    \node[font=\small, gray] at (2.4,-1.3) {p3: three copies at 0$^\circ$, 120$^\circ$, 240$^\circ$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{document}
```

> [!info] p3 Properties
> - **Lattice:** Hexagonal
> - **Rotation:** $120°$ (Order 3)
> - **Mirrors:** 0 | **Glides:** 0
> - Triangular/hexagonal repeating pinwheels without mirror symmetry.

#### p3m1 and p31m — $120°$ + 3 Mirrors

> [!info] p3m1 vs p31m
> Both groups have hexagonal lattice, $120°$ rotation, and 3 mirror axes. The difference is subtle:
> - **p3m1:** All rotation centers lie **on** the reflection lines.
> - **p31m:** Some rotation centers do **not** lie on the reflection lines.

---

### Sixth-Turn Groups (Order 6)

These groups have $60°$ rotational symmetry, the highest possible. Always use a **hexagonal lattice**.

#### p6 — $60°$ Rotation Only

> [!info] p6 Properties
> - **Lattice:** Hexagonal
> - **Rotation:** $60°$ (Order 6)
> - **Mirrors:** 0 | **Glides:** 0
> - Pure six-fold rotation, resembling spinning snowflakes without any mirrors.

#### p6m — $60°$ + 6 Mirrors (Highest Symmetry)

```tikz
\begin{document}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.65]
    % Hexagonal symmetry star
    \foreach \a in {0,60,120,180,240,300} {
        \draw[thick, blue!70!black] (0,0) -- ({1.8*cos(\a)},{1.8*sin(\a)});
    }
    \foreach \a in {0,60,120,180,240,300} {
        \draw[dashed, red!50] (0,0) -- ({2.2*cos(\a+30)},{2.2*sin(\a+30)});
    }
    % Rotation center
    \fill[blue!70!black] (0,0) circle (3pt);
    \node[font=\small, gray] at (0,-2.8) {p6m: 6-fold rotation + 6 mirrors (dashed red)};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{document}
```

> [!info] p6m Properties
> - **Lattice:** Hexagonal
> - **Rotation:** $60°$ (Order 6)
> - **Mirrors:** 6
> - The **highest symmetry group** of all 17. Found in perfect honeycomb structures and complex Islamic geometric patterns. Contains 2-fold, 3-fold, AND 6-fold rotation centers simultaneously.
