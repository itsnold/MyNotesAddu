## Propositional Functions

> [!definition] Propositional Function
> A **propositional function** (also called a **predicate** or **open sentence**) is a statement containing a variable, written $P(x)$, whose truth value depends on what value $x$ takes. The set of allowed values is called the **domain of discourse**.

Unlike a proposition (which is definitively True or False), a propositional function is neither true nor false until the variable gets a specific value.

> [!example] Propositional Function Example
> $Q(x): x + 5 = 10$ over the domain $\mathbb{Z}$ (integers).
>
> - $Q(3): 3 + 5 = 10$ → **False**
> - $Q(5): 5 + 5 = 10$ → **True**
> - $Q(-2): -2 + 5 = 10$ → **False**

> [!definition] Truth Set
> The **truth set** of $P(x)$ is the set of all values from the domain that make $P(x)$ true:
> $$\{x \in D \mid P(x) \text{ is true}\}$$
>
> For $Q(x): x + 5 = 10$ over $\mathbb{Z}$, the truth set is $\{5\}$.

> [!example] More Examples
> | Propositional Function | Domain | Truth Set |
> |---|---|---|
> | $P(x): x^2 = 4$ | $\mathbb{Z}$ | $\{-2, 2\}$ |
> | $R(x): x > 0$ | $\mathbb{R}$ | $(0, \infty)$ |
> | $S(x): x \text{ is prime and even}$ | $\mathbb{Z}^+$ | $\{2\}$ |
> | $T(x): x^2 < 0$ | $\mathbb{R}$ | $\emptyset$ |

## Quantifiers

Quantifiers turn propositional functions into propositions by specifying how many elements in the domain must satisfy the predicate.

### Universal Quantifier

> [!definition] Universal Quantifier ($\forall$)
> **"For all," "For every," "For each."**
>
> $$\forall x \in D,\ P(x)$$
>
> This statement is **true** if $P(x)$ holds for **every single element** in the domain $D$. It is **false** if even ONE counterexample exists.

> [!example] Universal Quantifier Examples
> - $\forall x \in \mathbb{R},\ x + 1 > x$ → **True** (adding 1 always produces something larger)
> - $\forall x \in \mathbb{R},\ x^2 \ge 0$ → **True** (squares are never negative for reals)
> - $\forall x \in \mathbb{Z},\ x^2 > 0$ → **False** (counterexample: $x = 0$, since $0^2 = 0 \not> 0$)

> [!warning] How to Disprove a Universal Statement
> To show $\forall x, P(x)$ is **false**, you only need to find **one** value of $x$ where $P(x)$ fails. This is called a **counterexample**.

### Existential Quantifier

> [!definition] Existential Quantifier ($\exists$)
> **"There exists," "For some," "There is at least one."**
>
> $$\exists x \in D,\ P(x)$$
>
> This statement is **true** if there is **at least one** element in the domain making $P(x)$ true. It is **false** only if NO element works.

> [!example] Existential Quantifier Examples
> - $\exists x \in \mathbb{Z},\ \sqrt{x} \in \mathbb{Z}$ → **True** (e.g., $x = 4$ gives $\sqrt{4} = 2 \in \mathbb{Z}$)
> - $\exists x \in \mathbb{R},\ x^2 = -1$ → **False** (no real number squared gives $-1$)
> - $\exists x \in \mathbb{Z},\ x + x = x$ → **True** ($x = 0$ works: $0 + 0 = 0$)

> [!tip] Comparing the Two Quantifiers
> | Aspect | Universal ($\forall$) | Existential ($\exists$) |
> |---|---|---|
> | Meaning | "All" | "At least one" |
> | True when | Every single element works | At least one element works |
> | False when | Even one counterexample exists | No element works at all |
> | To prove true | Check all elements (or use a general proof) | Find one working example |
> | To prove false | Find one counterexample | Show no element can work |

## Negating Quantified Statements

Negation flips the quantifier and negates the predicate. This is one of the most tested skills.

> [!abstract] The Negation Rules
> $$\neg(\forall x,\ P(x)) \equiv \exists x,\ \neg P(x)$$
> $$\neg(\exists x,\ P(x)) \equiv \forall x,\ \neg P(x)$$
>
> In words: the negation of "all are" is "some are not," and the negation of "some are" is "none are."

> [!info] Negation in Plain English
> | Original Statement | Negation |
> |---|---|
> | "All $X$ are $Y$" | "Some $X$ are not $Y$" |
> | "Some $X$ are $Y$" | "No $X$ are $Y$" |
> | "No $X$ are $Y$" | "Some $X$ are $Y$" |

> [!example] Negation Examples
> 1. **Statement:** "All successful brands invest in social media."
>    **Negation:** "Some successful brands do not invest in social media."
>
> 2. **Statement:** "No employees are late to meetings."
>    **Negation:** "Some employees are late to meetings."
>
> 3. **Statement:** "Some online learners attend physical classes."
>    **Negation:** "No online learners attend physical classes."
>
> 4. **Statement:** $\forall x \in \mathbb{R},\ x^2 \ge 0$
>    **Negation:** $\exists x \in \mathbb{R},\ x^2 < 0$

> [!warning] Common Mistake
> The negation of "All students passed" is **NOT** "All students failed."
>
> It IS: "Some students did not pass." (At least one failed, the rest might have passed.)
