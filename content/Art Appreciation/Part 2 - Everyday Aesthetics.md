# 🌿 Everyday Aesthetics

> [!summary] What this covers
> The idea that beauty and aesthetic experience aren't locked up in museums. They're all around us, in chores, coffee cups, and messy rooms. Also: Wabi-Sabi, functional beauty, and whether graffiti is art or vandalism.

## I. Historical Context

So how did aesthetics get so narrow?

> [!info]+ How aesthetics changed over time
> | Era | What happened |
> |-----|---------------|
> | **Early History** | Aesthetics covered everything: objects, activities, all of it |
> | **18th/19th Century** | Got narrowed down to just **Fine Arts** (painting, sculpture, museum stuff) |
> | **20th Century** | Philosophy focused on "What is Art?" and kept it separate from daily life |
> | **Late 20th Century to Now** | Return to **Everyday Aesthetics**: wait, maybe beauty is everywhere? |

> [!tip]+ The shift
> Everyday Aesthetics basically says: "Why are we only looking at things in museums? There's aesthetic experience in cooking dinner, organizing a shelf, or drinking coffee."

## II. Wabi-Sabi

This is a big one. Japanese aesthetic philosophy.

> [!definition]+ Wabi-Sabi (侘寂)
> Acceptance of **transience** and **imperfection**.
> 
> | Term | Meaning |
> |------|---------|
> | **Wabi** | Less is more. Rustic simplicity. Beauty in austerity. |
> | **Sabi** | Attentive melancholy. Appreciating the passage of time. |
> 
> > [!abstract]+ Core idea
> > Finding beauty in things that are:
> > 1. **Imperfect**
> > 2. **Impermanent**
> > 3. **Incomplete**

---

### Kintsugi: The Art of Golden Repair

> [!example]+ Kintsugi (金継ぎ)
> ![[attachments/kintsugi.png]]
> *Broken pottery repaired with gold lacquer*
> 
> - Instead of hiding the cracks, you fill them with **gold**
> - The breakage becomes part of the object's history
> - A repaired bowl is *more* beautiful than an unbroken one
> - This is Wabi-Sabi in practice: embrace the flaw, make it shine

> [!example]+ Nature Reclaiming
> ![[attachments/nature_reclaiming.png]]
> *Plants growing through abandoned architecture*
> 
> Same energy. The beauty of decay. Time winning, gently.

## III. The 6 Dimensions of Everyday Aesthetics

### 1. Broadening the Scope

> [!info]+ Beyond museums
> Everyday Aesthetics expands the territory to include:
> - **Nature** and environment
> - **Functional objects** (tools, furniture, kitchenware)
> - **Household chores** (cooking, cleaning, organizing)
> - **Routine activities** (commuting, eating, dressing)
> 
> The point is finding aesthetic experience in the *ordinary*.

---

### 2. Reevaluating Functionality

> [!abstract]+ Functional Beauty
> | Old thinking | New thinking |
> |--------------|--------------|
> | "Art can't be useful" | "Usefulness can *make* something beautiful" |
> | Disinterested contemplation only | Aesthetic value tied to how well it works |
> | Separate art from utility | A well-designed tool *is* beautiful |

> [!example]+ Examples
> - A kitchen knife that cuts perfectly and feels right in your hand
> - A chair that's so well-balanced you forget you're sitting
> - A mug that's warm and satisfying to hold

---

### 3. Active Participation vs. Passive Viewing

> [!info]+ Doing vs. looking
> | Traditional Art | Everyday Aesthetics |
> |-----------------|---------------------|
> | **Spectator**: stand back and look | **Agent**: doing stuff, engaging |
> | Keep your distance | Get your hands dirty |
> | Contemplate | Participate |

> [!warning]+ The pedestal problem
> If you take an everyday object and put it on a museum pedestal, you *destroy* its everydayness. The aesthetic experience is in the *use*.

> [!example]+ Aesthetics of activity
> - The rhythm of chopping vegetables
> - The satisfaction of a perfectly made bed
> - The almost meditative quality of washing dishes

---

### 4. Social Aesthetics

> [!info]+ Aesthetics of human interaction
> How you interact with people has aesthetic dimensions:
> - **Facial expressions** and eye contact
> - **Tone of voice** and rhythm of speech
> - **Gestures** and posture
> - **How you handle objects** in front of others
> 
> Think about it: the way someone pours tea, hands you something, or greets you. These have a *feel*.

> [!tip]+ Social gracefulness
> Being graceful with people is an aesthetic quality. Awkwardness is *aesthetically* uncomfortable, not just socially.

---

### 5. Beyond Beauty and Sublimity

> [!abstract]+ More aesthetic categories
> Traditional aesthetics only cared about **The Beautiful** and **The Sublime**.
> 
> Everyday Aesthetics says there's more:
> 
> | Quality | What it means |
> |---------|---------------|
> | **The Boring** | Understated, background-level experience |
> | **The Plain** | Simple, quiet, unadorned |
> | **The Messy** | Chaotic, lived-in, organic |
> | **The Cute** | Small, endearing, childlike |
> | **The Cozy** | Warm, intimate, comfortable |
> 
> All of these trigger aesthetic responses. They count.

---

### 6. Negative Aesthetic Qualities

> [!info]+ What about ugliness?
> Everyday Aesthetics doesn't pretend ugly things don't exist. It engages with them.
> 
> > [!tip]+ Two ways to respond to something ugly
> > 
> > **1. Artistic lens**
> > Look at the ugly thing *as if* it were art:
> > - Finding beauty in a dying flower
> > - Seeing poetry in urban decay
> > - Appreciating the raw honesty of imperfection
> > 
> > **2. Motivated action**
> > The ugliness makes you *do* something:
> > 
> > ```
> > Messy room -> Discomfort -> Clean it -> Order restored
> > ```
> > 
> > The negative aesthetic quality motivates transformation.

## IV. Graffiti: Art or Pollution?

This is a good case study because graffiti sits right on the border.

> [!example]+ Graffiti as visual pollution
> ![[attachments/graffiti_vandalism.png]]
> 
> When it looks like this:
> - Just **vandalism** of property
> - No consent, no permission
> - An eyesore in the urban environment

> [!example]+ Graffiti as social commentary
> ![[attachments/banksy.png]]
> *Banksy-style street art*
> 
> But when it looks like this:
> - Conveys **political messages**
> - Gives voice to people who don't have gallery access
> - Challenges complacency
> - Transforms forgotten spaces

> [!example]+ Lumad Rights Murals
> ![[attachments/lumad_mural.png]]
> *Philippine indigenous rights advocacy*
> 
> Art as activism: using public walls to raise awareness about indigenous peoples' struggles. Is it vandalism or resistance? Depends who you ask.

## V. Criticisms of Everyday Aesthetics

> [!warning]+ Three problems people bring up
> 
> **1. Subjective relativism**
> - "My experience of doing dishes is totally different from yours"
> - If everything is personal, how do we even have a critical discussion?
> 
> **2. Self-indulgence**
> - Without standards, it just becomes "I like this"
> - Risks turning aesthetics into pure personal preference
> 
> **3. Loss of authority**
> - If *everything* is aesthetic, does expertise matter?
> - Do artists and critics still have a role?

These are fair criticisms. The field is still figuring out answers.

## VI. Developments in Art

Some art movements that line up with Everyday Aesthetics:

> [!abstract]+ Art movements that get it
> 
> | Movement | What's happening |
> |----------|------------------|
> | **Conceptual Art** | The idea matters more than the physical object |
> | **Environmental Art** | Art merged with landscapes and nature |
> | **Performance Art** | The artist's body and actions *are* the art |
> | **Socially Engaged Art** | Artists collaborating with regular people in everyday activities |
> 
> > [!example]+ Socially engaged examples
> > - Artists working on community farming projects
> > - Collaborative cooking as art practice
> > - Neighborhood beautification where everyone participates

---

> [!abstract]+ The point of all this
> Everyday Aesthetics teaches that:
> - **Beauty is everywhere**: stop only looking in museums
> - **Function can be beautiful**: usefulness adds value
> - **Doing is aesthetic**: not just looking
> - **Social interactions** have sensory weight
> - **Negative qualities** (boring, messy, plain) are valid experiences too
> - Art is circling back to **ordinary life** where it started
